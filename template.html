BOPTEST results, every test period

Each block is a BOPTEST test period after a 7-day warm-up, scored by BOPTEST's own KPI calculator. Chicago is the official test case (14 days each). New Delhi swaps in ISHRAE-2014 weather with the Chicago-sized plant unchanged; its periods are shorter because the simulation stalled later in those windows, and the monsoon Comfort run did not finish. Energy is HVAC electricity per m² of floor. Thermal discomfort is kelvin-hours outside the 20–24 °C band while occupied (summed over zones). Air-quality discomfort is ppm-hours above the 894 ppm CO₂ limit. Eco runs night pre-cooling, supply-air reset and CO₂-led fresh air. Comfort adds the comfort guard.

What the same rules find in a real office

CU-BEMS is an open dataset from a 7-storey office building at Chulalongkorn University, Bangkok: one-minute readings of every split AC, lighting and plug circuit plus zone temperature, for all of 2019. Bangkok's climate is close to Mumbai's or Chennai's, and its split ACs are common in Indian offices too.

Anukul's Sense step treats a zone as occupied when its lights and plugs draw clearly more than their own night-time standby. It then asks two questions: was the AC running in an empty zone, and was it running while the zone sat below 23 °C?

Overcooling saving is an estimate. It uses BEE's figure of about 6% of AC electricity per 1 °C of set-point raise (upper) and half that (lower), applied up to 4 °C below 24 °C. The empty-zone figure is measured energy in intervals with no sign of people. Zone sensors may sit near AC outlets, so overcooling may be overstated.
